abstract: Thermoacoustic instabilities arise when fluctuations in heat release couple with acoustic waves in the combustor, forming a feedback loop that can amplify pressure oscillations and lead to severe damage or even catastrophic failure. The response of the flame’s overall unsteady heat release rate (HRR) to acoustic or equivalence ratio perturbations can be characterized by flame transfer function (FTF). Accurate experimental characterization of HRR is essential, yet direct measurements remain impractical because they require simultaneous determination of multiple species and temperatures.

 In practice, chemiluminescence intensity (e.g., CH* and OH* in hydrocarbon fuels) is a widely used indicator of HRR in fully premixed flames. However, this approach becomes less reliable under partially premixed conditions, where equivalence ratio fluctuations strongly influence emission intensity. In this context, the FTF obtained through chemiluminescence measurements will be referred to as the optical FTF. Developing reliable methods to construct FTFs under these conditions remains a pressing challenge.

 Motivated by the challenges discussed above, this study evaluates a correction strategy for the optical FTF, using chemiluminescence intensity and flame surface area to construct FTFs in an acoustically forced, partially premixed hydrogen flame.
